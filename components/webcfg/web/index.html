Safety

When the flight controller stops the recording — you disarm, or you crash — the camera carries on for this long before it actually stops. A crash disarms the quad, and the seconds you want are the ones after that: where it went, and what it landed on. Arming again during the countdown just carries on recording, so it costs you nothing on a normal landing. The OSD counts down so you can see it happening rather than wondering why the camera is still running.

Pressing the button or your record switch stops immediately — this only ever delays the automatic stop, never you.

Warnings

The state field flashes a warning instead of REC or IDLE when something is wrong, and the module's LED blinks fast when it has been asked to record and is not. The most important one wins: NO SD, then CAM HOT, then NOT REC, then the two below.
Either at zero switches that warning off. NOT REC is not a threshold and cannot be switched off — it is the reason this module exists.

Radio power

Uses the power above while you are disarmed, and drops to Low for as long as the quad is armed. Setting up wants reach — the camera may be across the room. Flying does not: it is centimetres from the camera and millimetres from your receiver, where being loud buys nothing and costs the link that matters. With this on you can leave the setting above on High without carrying it into the air.

Setup switch

Opens this page from your transmitter, for when the module is buried in the frame and you cannot reach its button.
How long the switch must be held before the OSD says READY. Zero is ready the instant the channel crosses — you still commit by dropping the switch.
The switch only ever opens setup. You leave it with Done & restart at the bottom of this page, or by arming — nothing else on the aircraft will drop you out of the page while you are using it.

Setup is refused while armed, and arming leaves it, because a module in setup has no camera link at all: an aircraft that took off in it would record nothing and say nothing about why.
